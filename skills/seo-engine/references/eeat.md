# EEAT Rules: authorship and trust signals for every site

This is a **blocking checklist**, same tier as `writing-rules.md`. No article or page is delivered
unless it passes every rule below, in addition to the writing-rules gates.

## Authorship

- **Byline is always "Written by the WildScout Team"**, linking to `/about`. Never invent an
  individual author name, a persona, credentials, or a job title for a byline. The team is the
  author of record, full stop.
- **Never write a personal anecdote or first-person testing claim.** No "I tested this on my last
  trip", no "we took this out for three nights in the Sierras", no invented dates, locations, or
  outcomes attributed to hands-on use the site did not actually perform. If a draft contains any
  sentence implying an individual staff member personally used, tested, or field-tried a product,
  rewrite it before shipping. This is a hard fail, not a style note.
- The site's own honest framing (see the About page) is: research-based curation, not hands-on
  field testing. Every claim in an article must be consistent with that framing. Write from that
  posture: "manufacturer specs show...", "owners consistently report...", "independent testing
  data puts...", never "we found..." or "in our testing...".

## Sourcing every claim

- Every factual claim (a spec, a rating, a price, a durability or performance claim) must trace to
  one of: manufacturer specs/data, aggregated verified owner reviews (e.g. retailer review
  aggregates, forum consensus across multiple independent posters), or an independently published
  test standard (e.g. ASTM ratings). Do not present a single anonymous review as consensus.
- When a claim comes from owner reviews rather than a spec sheet, say so in the text ("owners
  report...", "a common complaint in reviews is...") rather than stating it as the site's own
  finding.
- This sits on top of, not instead of, the existing accuracy rule in `writing-rules.md`: every
  price/stat/date/product claim is still verified live during the run, never from memory.

## Affiliate disclosure

- **Every article includes the affiliate disclosure line near the top** (before or immediately
  after the opening quick-answer section, not buried at the bottom): a short, plain-language note
  that the site earns a commission on some links at no extra cost to the reader, and that this does
  not influence which products get recommended. Match the wording already on the About and
  Privacy Policy pages rather than inventing new phrasing each time.
- The disclosure is a genuine sentence, not a legal-boilerplate wall of text: one or two sentences
  is enough for the in-article placement.

## Self-review addition

Add these checks to the existing self-review gate in `writing-rules.md` before saving:

- [ ] Byline is "Written by the WildScout Team" linking to `/about` (or the site's build already
      renders this automatically at the layout level; either way, confirm no competing byline was
      written into the body).
- [ ] Zero first-person or named-individual testing claims anywhere in the draft (title, body, FAQ).
- [ ] Every spec/rating/price/performance claim is attributed to manufacturer data, verified owner
      reviews, or an independent test standard, either explicitly in the sentence or consistent with
      the site's sourcing norm.
- [ ] Affiliate disclosure line present near the top of the article, matching the site's standard wording.
